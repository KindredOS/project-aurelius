## Introduction to Statics and Dynamics

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces the study of forces and motion. Prompt: What causes things to move or stay still?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Statics and dynamics are parts of physics that help us understand how objects behave. Statics looks at forces that keep things still or in balance. Dynamics studies what happens when things start moving. Together, they help us explain everything from buildings standing tall to rockets blasting off.

## Equilibrium and Balance

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores the idea of balance in systems. Prompt: Can you find examples of balance in the world around you?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Equilibrium happens when all the forces on an object cancel each other out. That means the object isn’t moving or changing motion. Engineers use this idea when designing bridges or buildings to make sure everything stays stable.

## Understanding Net Force

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section focuses on how forces combine to create motion or stability. Prompt: What happens when multiple forces act on the same object?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Net force is the total of all the forces acting on an object. If the forces are equal and opposite, the object stays still. If one force is stronger, the object will move in that direction. Understanding net force helps us figure out how things will move—or not move.

## Free-Body Diagrams

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces how to sketch and label the forces acting on an object. Prompt: How can drawing a picture help you solve a physics problem?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

A free-body diagram is a simple sketch that shows all the forces acting on an object. It’s like giving each force a name and direction. These diagrams make it easier to solve problems about motion and balance.

## Dynamics and Motion

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains how forces cause objects to accelerate. Prompt: What makes something speed up, slow down, or change direction?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Dynamics looks at what happens when forces cause motion. Newton’s laws help us understand how things speed up, slow down, or change direction. Whether it’s a soccer ball or a car, dynamics explains how movement works.

## Friction and Resistance

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section discusses how surfaces and materials affect motion. Prompt: How does friction help or stop things from moving?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Friction is a force that resists motion between two surfaces. It can be helpful, like when brakes stop a bike, or it can slow things down, like shoes dragging on the floor. Engineers must think about friction when designing anything that moves.

## Energy Transfer

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores how motion involves changing forms of energy. Prompt: Where does energy go when something starts or stops moving?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

When something moves, energy changes form. A roller coaster at the top of a hill has potential energy. As it goes down, that energy becomes kinetic—energy of motion. Studying these changes helps us understand engines, machines, and even our own bodies.
