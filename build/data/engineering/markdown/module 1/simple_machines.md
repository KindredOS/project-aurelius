## Introduction to Simple Machines

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces simple machines and why they matter. Prompt: How can simple tools make tough jobs easier?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Simple machines are basic tools that help us do work by changing the direction or strength of a force. These machines don’t have many moving parts, but they can make lifting, pushing, or pulling much easier. Understanding how they work helps us build better tools and solve everyday problems.

## Levers

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains how levers help lift and move objects. Prompt: Can you think of a lever you use at home or school?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

A lever is a straight bar that pivots on a point called a fulcrum. Depending on where the load and effort are placed, levers can multiply force. Think of a seesaw, crowbar, or scissors—all of these use levers to make work easier.

## Pulleys

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces pulleys and how they lift loads with less effort. Prompt: Where might you find pulleys in everyday life?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Pulleys use wheels and ropes to lift heavy objects. By changing the direction of the force, pulleys make lifting easier. Add more wheels, and you use even less effort. You’ll find pulleys in flagpoles, cranes, and even elevators.

## Inclined Planes

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section describes how ramps reduce the effort needed to move things upward. Prompt: How do ramps help us move heavy things?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

An inclined plane is a flat surface tilted at an angle. Instead of lifting something straight up, you can push or roll it along a ramp. This spreads the effort over a longer distance, making heavy lifting much easier.

## Wheels and Axles

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section looks at how wheels reduce friction and help with motion. Prompt: How do wheels help things move more easily?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

A wheel and axle is a circular frame (the wheel) that turns around a central rod (the axle). This setup reduces friction and makes it easier to move loads. Cars, carts, and doorknobs all use wheels and axles to work more efficiently.

\[interactive element]

## Screws

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores how screws use twisting force to hold things or lift. Prompt: Where do you see screws being used around you?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

A screw is an inclined plane wrapped around a rod. As it turns, it pulls things together or lifts them up. Screws are used in bottle caps, clamps, and jacks—and are essential for building and fastening materials securely.

\[interactive element]

## Wedges

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains how wedges split or cut things apart. Prompt: What tools use wedges to get work done?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

A wedge is a device that comes to a point and forces things apart. It’s like two inclined planes joined together. Knives, axes, and chisels are all wedges that help cut, split, or shape materials by applying force to a narrow edge.

## Real-World Engineering

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section connects simple machines to real-world inventions. Prompt: Can you think of a machine that uses more than one simple machine?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Many tools and machines we use every day are built from combinations of simple machines. A bicycle uses wheels and axles, levers in the pedals, and gears to transfer force. Engineers combine these elements to create complex tools that solve practical problems.
