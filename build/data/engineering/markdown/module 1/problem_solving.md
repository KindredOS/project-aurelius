## Introduction to Problem Solving

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces engineering as a way to solve problems. Prompt: What steps do you take when you face a challenge you don’t know how to solve?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Problem-solving is at the heart of engineering. Engineers use creativity and logic to design solutions that work in the real world. They break problems into smaller parts, gather information, and test ideas until they find something that works. This approach can be used for anything—from fixing a bike to designing a rocket.

## Modeling the Problem

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores how sketches and diagrams help us understand problems better. Prompt: Why is it helpful to draw or model a problem before solving it?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Modeling means creating a simplified version of the problem—using drawings, simulations, or even physical prototypes. It helps engineers visualize challenges and test possible solutions before building the real thing. Good models can save time and resources.

## Dimensional Analysis

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section teaches how to check and convert measurements in a problem. Prompt: Why do you need to keep track of units when solving problems?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Dimensional analysis is a method that uses units to check whether calculations make sense. By converting between units and making sure measurements match, engineers can avoid errors. It’s a powerful tool for making accurate predictions and verifying results.

## Logical Reasoning

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section shows how step-by-step thinking leads to better solutions. Prompt: How does logic help you decide what to do next?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Logical reasoning means using facts, rules, and patterns to make decisions. Engineers use logic to plan each step of a solution, check their assumptions, and troubleshoot when things don’t go as expected. Logical thinking keeps problem-solving organized and efficient.

## Breaking It Down

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section encourages students to divide big problems into smaller parts. Prompt: Have you ever solved a big challenge by tackling one part at a time?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

When a problem seems too big, breaking it down into smaller pieces makes it more manageable. Each smaller problem can be solved step-by-step. Engineers often work on one part of a larger system at a time, then combine the solutions together.

## Testing and Revising

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains how engineers use trial and error to improve ideas. Prompt: What can you learn when your first idea doesn’t work?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Testing is how engineers learn what works and what doesn’t. Even failed attempts give clues for better designs. Revising based on feedback and data helps create stronger, safer, and more effective solutions.

## Communicating Ideas

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section shows why sharing designs and feedback is important. Prompt: How can you explain your solution so others can understand it?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Good communication helps others understand your ideas and improve them. Engineers use sketches, reports, presentations, and models to share their thinking. Clear communication builds teamwork and leads to better results.

## Real-World Applications

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section connects engineering strategies to daily life. Prompt: Where in your life could you use problem-solving like an engineer?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Engineering problem-solving isn't just for labs and factories. It’s useful in daily life—from planning a trip to designing a school project. Anytime you face a challenge, you can think like an engineer: observe, model, test, and improve.
