## Introduction to Force, Motion, and Energy

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces how forces and energy affect motion. Prompt: What do you think causes things to start or stop moving?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Everything that moves is affected by forces and energy. Force is a push or pull. Energy is the ability to do work. When these two ideas work together, they explain how objects move, change direction, speed up, or slow down. Learning about them helps us understand the world in motion.

## Newton's Laws of Motion

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains the basic rules of motion. Prompt: How do Newton’s Laws explain the way things move?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Sir Isaac Newton described three laws that explain how objects move. The first says things keep doing what they’re doing unless a force changes it. The second connects force, mass, and acceleration. The third says every action has an equal and opposite reaction. These laws are the foundation of physics.

## Types of Energy

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces different forms of energy. Prompt: What are some types of energy you’ve used or seen today?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Energy comes in many forms—kinetic (motion), potential (stored), thermal (heat), light, and more. Each type can be transformed into another. For example, a toaster changes electrical energy into thermal energy. Understanding energy helps us use it wisely and safely.

## Mechanical Advantage

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores how tools can make work easier. Prompt: How do simple machines help us use less effort?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Mechanical advantage means using a tool to multiply your force. Levers, pulleys, and gears let you lift heavy objects or move them with less effort. Engineers use these ideas to design machines that save energy and increase power.

## Torque and Rotation

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces twisting forces and turning motion. Prompt: What happens when you twist something instead of push it?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Torque is the force that causes something to spin. It depends on how much force you use and how far it is from the pivot point. From turning a wrench to spinning a bicycle wheel, torque helps explain rotation in machines and everyday life.

## Friction and Resistance

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains how surfaces affect motion. Prompt: When does friction help you, and when does it slow you down?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Friction is the resistance between two surfaces rubbing together. It helps us walk without slipping but also slows things down. Engineers must balance friction—adding it for safety or reducing it for speed.

## Energy Transfer and Conservation

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores how energy moves and changes forms. Prompt: What happens to energy when something moves or changes?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Energy can’t be created or destroyed—only transferred or changed from one form to another. A bouncing ball turns potential energy into kinetic energy and back again. Engineers use this principle to design efficient systems.
