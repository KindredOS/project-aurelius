## What Is Ethics in Science?

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces the importance of ethics in research and experimentation. Prompt: Why do scientists need to follow ethical rules when doing experiments?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Ethics in science is like playing a game with clear rules — everyone must be fair, honest, and careful.

- **Simple Explanation**: Ethics means doing the right thing in science — not hurting people or nature, and telling the truth about your work.
- **Metaphor**: Imagine science as a team sport. If one player cheats, the whole team loses.
- **Real-World Example**: A scientist testing a new medicine must make sure it’s safe before people use it.
- **Application**: Good ethics protect lives and build trust.

## Honesty and Integrity

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section discusses the role of honesty in reporting results. Prompt: What could happen if scientists faked or changed their data?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Being truthful in science is as important as the facts themselves.

- **Simple Explanation**: Scientists must never lie about their results.
- **Metaphor**: It’s like baking — if you say you used sugar but used salt, no one will trust your recipe.
- **Real-World Example**: A climate scientist who lies about temperature data could cause bad policies.
- **Application**: Lies in science can harm real people.
- **Reframed** (for [interests]): Whether you're designing a robot or writing a story, being honest about how something works or how a plot unfolds is key to gaining trust.

## Avoiding Bias

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains how scientists try to prevent personal beliefs from affecting their work. Prompt: What are some ways scientists can reduce bias in their experiments?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Science only works if others can repeat it and get the same answer.

- **Simple Explanation**: Other scientists should be able to copy your experiment and get the same results.
- **Metaphor**: Like a recipe — if no one else can bake the same cake, the recipe may be wrong.
- **Real-World Example**: If a new drug works only once and never again, it’s not reliable.
- **Application**: Clear steps and careful notes make science stronger.
- **Reframed** (for [interests]): Think about your favorite hobby in [interests] — if others can't do it the way you do, maybe your method isn’t clear enough to share.

This section focuses on making experiments clear enough that others can repeat them. Prompt: Why is it important for experiments to be reproducible?

- **Application**: Studying these failures helps protect future lives.
