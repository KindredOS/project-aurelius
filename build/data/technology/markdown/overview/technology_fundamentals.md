## Technology Fundamentals

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces the basic principles behind how technology works.
**Prompt:** What are some simple machines or tools you use every day, and how do they help you?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Technology is the use of tools, techniques, and systems to solve problems and improve human life. At its core, technology builds on physical science, engineering, and human creativity to design solutions for everyday challenges.

## Simple Machines

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores basic mechanical devices that make work easier.
**Prompt:** Can you think of examples of pulleys, levers, or inclined planes in your home or school?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Simple machines include levers, pulleys, wheels and axles, inclined planes, screws, and wedges. These tools reduce the amount of force needed to move objects and are the building blocks of more complex technologies.

## Energy and Power

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section looks at the energy sources that power technology.
**Prompt:** What’s an example of how energy is transformed to power a device?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Technology often transforms one form of energy into another—like batteries storing chemical energy or solar panels capturing sunlight. Understanding energy flow helps us use resources more efficiently and design better systems.

## Materials and Properties

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section discusses the materials used in technology and why they matter.
**Prompt:** Why might engineers choose one material over another for a project?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Technologies are made of materials chosen for their properties—like strength, flexibility, or conductivity. Metals, plastics, ceramics, and composites all play different roles in building safe, effective tools and devices.

## Systems Thinking

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces systems as interconnected parts working together.
**Prompt:** What’s a system you’ve used that combines many parts to do something useful?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

A system is a group of components that interact to perform a function—like a car engine, a computer, or even a school. Systems thinking helps us troubleshoot problems, design improvements, and understand complexity.

## Inputs, Processes, Outputs

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section breaks down how technologies operate through input, process, and output.
**Prompt:** What are the inputs, processes, and outputs of a vending machine or microwave?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

All technologies follow a similar logic: they take inputs (like energy or commands), perform a process (like heating or sorting), and produce outputs (like cooked food or a result). Recognizing this flow is key to understanding how tech works.

## Design and Prototyping

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section covers how ideas become working models through design and testing.
**Prompt:** Have you ever made or improved something through trial and error?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Design is a creative process involving planning, building, testing, and refining. Engineers and inventors use prototypes—early models—to test ideas, find flaws, and develop functional products.

## Maintenance and Sustainability

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains how we keep technology working and reduce its impact.
**Prompt:** What are ways to extend the life of a device or use it more sustainably?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Technology needs upkeep—like software updates, repairs, or recycling—to stay useful and eco-friendly. Sustainable design considers how a product is made, used, and disposed of, aiming to minimize harm to people and the planet.
