## What Is Exponential Growth?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces exponential growth as rapid, repeated multiplication.  
Prompt: How is doubling every day different from adding 2 every day?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Exponential growth** happens when a quantity increases by the same factor over equal time intervals.  
- Example: y = 2ˣ doubles each step (1, 2, 4, 8, 16…).  
- This is faster than linear growth (1, 3, 5, 7…).  

Exponential models describe rapid change like populations, money, or technology.

## What Is Exponential Decay?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains decay as the reverse of growth.  
Prompt: How is losing half each time different from subtracting 5 each time?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Exponential decay** happens when a quantity decreases by the same fraction over time.  
- Example: y = (1/2)ˣ shrinks quickly (1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125…).  
- Radioactive half-life and depreciation of value are examples.  

Decay models help us understand processes that fade or wear out.

## What Are Logarithms?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces logarithms as the inverse of exponents.  
Prompt: If 10³ = 1000, then log₁₀(1000) = ?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **logarithm** answers the question: “What exponent makes this true?”  
- log₁₀(1000) = 3 because 10³ = 1000.  
- Logs undo exponentials, just like subtraction undoes addition.  

Logarithms are used to solve for unknown exponents.

## Logarithmic Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows where logarithms are useful.  
Prompt: Why might scientists use a log scale instead of regular numbers?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Logarithmic models describe situations that grow quickly at first but then level off.  
- Example: sound intensity (decibels).  
- Example: earthquake strength (Richter scale).  
- Example: cooling of objects.  

Logarithms help compress very large ranges of data into manageable scales.

## Comparing Exponential and Logarithmic Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section compares how they behave.  
Prompt: How does exponential growth look compared to logarithmic growth on a graph?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Exponential functions rise (or fall) rapidly.  
- Logarithmic functions rise quickly at first but then slow down.  
- They are inverses: if y = aˣ, then logₐ(y) = x.  

Together, they describe many processes of growth and change.

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects exponential and logarithmic models to the real world.  
Prompt: Where do you see doubling, decay, or log scales in real life?
