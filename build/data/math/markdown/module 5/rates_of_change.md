## What Is Rate of Change?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces rate of change as how one quantity changes compared to another.  
Prompt: If you drive 60 miles in 2 hours, how fast are you going?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **rate of change** compares the difference in one variable to the difference in another.  
- Example: Speed = distance ÷ time.  
- Example: Slope of a line = rise ÷ run.  

Rates of change tell us how fast or slow something is changing.

## Average Rate of Change

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how to calculate change over an interval.  
Prompt: How much did the temperature change per hour if it rose from 50° to 65° in 3 hours?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Average rate of change** = (change in y) ÷ (change in x).  
- Formula: (f(b) – f(a)) ÷ (b – a).  
- Example: (65 – 50) ÷ (3 – 0) = 15 ÷ 3 = 5° per hour.  

It shows the overall change between two points.

## Instantaneous Rate of Change

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces the idea of instantaneous change.  
Prompt: How is checking your car’s speedometer different from averaging your trip speed?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

An **instantaneous rate of change** tells how fast something is changing at a single moment.  
- Example: The speedometer shows your exact speed right now.  
- This idea leads to **derivatives** in calculus.  

It’s like zooming in to see change at one point.

## Graphical Interpretation

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how graphs show rates of change.  
Prompt: What does the slope of a line or curve tell us about change?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- On a straight line, slope = constant rate of change.  
- On a curve, slope changes along the graph.  
- Tangent lines show instantaneous slope at one point.  

Graphs make rates of change visible.

## Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects rates of change to real-world contexts.  
Prompt: Where do you use rates of change outside of math class?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Physics: speed, acceleration, velocity.  
- Economics: rate of inflation, profit changes.  
- Medicine: drug dosage decay in the body.  
- Biology: population growth rates.  

Rates of change explain how systems evolve and prepare us for calculus.
