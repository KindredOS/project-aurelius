## What Is Applied Modeling?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces applied modeling as using math to solve practical, interdisciplinary problems.  
Prompt: How can math help us design, plan, or predict something in the real world?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Applied modeling** means taking mathematical tools—equations, graphs, simulations—and using them to solve real-world challenges. It’s where abstract math meets practical decision-making.

## Modeling Across Disciplines

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how modeling connects to science, engineering, and beyond.  
Prompt: How might a scientist, engineer, or artist use math modeling in their work?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- **Science**: modeling disease spread or chemical reactions.  
- **Engineering**: testing bridge strength or traffic flow.  
- **Art and Design**: scaling patterns or modeling light and perspective.  
- **Business**: forecasting sales or optimizing supply chains.  

Different fields use math modeling to make sense of complex systems.

## Strengths and Limits of Applied Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section emphasizes the balance between usefulness and accuracy.  
Prompt: Why can’t models always predict the future exactly?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Models simplify reality—they capture important features but leave out details.  
- **Strength**: They make big problems manageable.  
- **Limit**: They depend on assumptions, and real life can change.  

Knowing the limits helps us use models wisely.
