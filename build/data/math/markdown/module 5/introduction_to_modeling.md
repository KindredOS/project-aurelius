## What Is Mathematical Modeling?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces modeling as a way to describe real-world situations with math.  
Prompt: How can an equation or graph tell the story of a real situation?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Mathematical modeling** is the process of taking a real problem, representing it with math, and using that model to make predictions or decisions.  
- Example: Using distance = rate × time to plan a trip.  
- Example: Modeling population growth with an exponential function.  

Models turn real situations into solvable math problems.

## The Modeling Cycle

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains the steps of building and refining a model.  
Prompt: What steps would you take to turn a word problem into a math equation?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

The modeling cycle includes:  
1. Identify the problem.  
2. Make assumptions and choose variables.  
3. Build a mathematical representation (equation, graph, or diagram).  
4. Analyze and solve.  
5. Interpret the results in context.  
6. Refine if necessary.  

It’s an ongoing loop of testing and improving.

## Choosing the Right Model

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains that different problems need different models.  
Prompt: Why might a linear model work for one situation but not another?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- **Linear models**: good for constant rates of change.  
- **Quadratic models**: good for curved paths (like a ball’s arc).  
- **Exponential models**: good for rapid growth or decay.  

The best model matches the behavior of the real-world situation.

## Strengths and Limitations of Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section highlights why models are helpful but imperfect.  
Prompt: Why can’t a math model always match real life exactly?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Models are simplifications—they capture key ideas but leave out small details.  
- Strength: Models help us make predictions and decisions quickly.  
- Limitation: They rely on assumptions and can break down if conditions change.  

Understanding limitations makes us better problem solvers.

## Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects modeling to the world outside of math class.  
Prompt: Where do people use math models in real life?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Scientists model climate change and disease spread.  
- Engineers model bridges, buildings, and machines.  
- Businesses model profit, costs, and growth.  
- Students model grades, schedules, and goals.  

Modeling is everywhere—it’s how math describes and improves the world.
