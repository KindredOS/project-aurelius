## What Are Word Problems?

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces the concept of word problems and why they are important in math. Prompt: Why do you think math problems are sometimes written as stories?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Word problems use everyday situations to frame math questions as short stories. They help students see how math applies to real life, such as planning a party or figuring out how long a trip will take. Instead of just solving equations, you're solving real-world puzzles.

## Reading Carefully Matters

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains how to read and understand a word problem step by step. Prompt: What clues should you look for when reading a word problem?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Solving a word problem starts with reading it carefully. Look for key details like numbers, units (minutes, dollars, miles), and what is being asked. Underline important words and draw diagrams if it helps.

## Visualizing the Problem

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores using drawings, charts, and diagrams to understand word problems better. Prompt: How can pictures help you solve math problems?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Turning words into visuals can make complex problems clearer. Try drawing a picture, making a chart, or using a number line. This can help you organize your thinking and spot the path to the solution.

## Choosing the Right Operation

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section teaches how to identify which math operation (add, subtract, multiply, divide) to use. Prompt: What words give clues about the math you need to do?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Word problems often contain hints like "total," "left over," "each," or "split." These clue words point to operations: "total" suggests addition, while "split" may mean division. Identifying them helps you know what to do next.

## Multi-Step Problems

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces problems that require more than one step or operation. Prompt: Why is it important to plan your steps when solving a long word problem?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Some problems need two or more steps to solve. For example, you might need to subtract, then multiply. It's helpful to make a plan: list what you know, decide what you need, and tackle each step in order.

## Estimating First

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section highlights the power of estimation in solving and checking answers. Prompt: How can estimating help you solve a problem more confidently?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Estimating means making a smart guess. Before solving, try estimating your answer to see what range it should fall in. It helps you catch mistakes and feel more confident about your work.

\[interactive element]

## Strategies for Success

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section shares helpful strategies students can use when stuck. Prompt: What do you do when you're stuck on a problem?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

When you're stuck, reread the problem, try different visuals, estimate again, or work backward from the answer. Sometimes talking it out with a friend helps, too. Practice builds confidence.

## Real-World Applications

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section shows how word problem skills are useful in life. Prompt: Can you think of a time you solved a real-life problem using math?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Word problems mirror real decisions—like budgeting for lunch, splitting a bill, or planning a schedule. These skills help you become a smart problem-solver in daily life.
