## What Is Representation in Math?

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces representation as a way to show mathematical ideas. Prompt: How do pictures, symbols, or objects help you understand math?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

In math, representation means using models, drawings, symbols, or words to express ideas. These tools help us see patterns, solve problems, and explain our thinking. Whether it’s a graph, equation, or block model, representations make math clearer and more visual.

\[interactive element]

## Types of Representations

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains the different ways math can be represented. Prompt: Can you think of a time when using a picture or graph helped you solve a problem?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Math can be shown in many forms: visual (charts, graphs), symbolic (equations, formulas), verbal (descriptions), physical (manipulatives), or numeric (tables, numbers). Each type helps us look at problems from new angles and find different solutions.

\[interactive element]

## Choosing the Right Representation

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section helps students think about when and why to choose a specific representation. Prompt: How do you decide which type of representation to use?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Some problems are easier to understand with a picture; others make more sense with a formula. Choosing the right representation depends on the question and what you're trying to figure out. Good math thinkers try different ways until one works best.

## Representations and Problem Solving

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section shows how switching representations can help solve problems. Prompt: Have you ever solved a problem by changing the way you looked at it?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Sometimes we get stuck on a problem because we’re looking at it one way. Switching to a graph or drawing might reveal something new. Changing representations can spark insight and make hard problems easier to tackle.

\[interactive element]

## Misleading Representations

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section warns that representations can sometimes be confusing or misleading. Prompt: What could go wrong if a chart or graph is unclear or incorrect?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Not all representations are helpful. A confusing graph, out-of-scale drawing, or missing labels can lead to mistakes. It’s important to question and double-check what a representation is really showing.

## Representation and Communication

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores how representations help us explain our thinking to others. Prompt: How can a good visual help someone else understand your math?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

When we show our work with a diagram or chart, it’s easier for others to follow our ideas. Representations help us communicate clearly and support group problem solving, class discussions, and presentations.

## Representation in the Real World

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section connects math representation to everyday life. Prompt: Where have you seen graphs, diagrams, or models outside of school?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

From weather maps to sports stats, representations are all around us. They help businesses track money, doctors explain treatments, and scientists share results. Knowing how to read and use these tools is a key life skill.

## Becoming a Skilled Math Communicator

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section encourages students to build confidence in using representations to explain ideas. Prompt: What is one way you can practice using representations to show your math thinking?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

You don’t have to be perfect—just start by showing your thinking clearly. Use drawings, graphs, or charts when you explain your work. The more you practice, the more confident you’ll become in showing what you know.
