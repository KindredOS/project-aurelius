## What Is Geometry?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces geometry as the study of shapes and space.  
Prompt: How does geometry help us describe the world around us?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Geometry** is the part of math that deals with shapes, sizes, and how things fit together in space.  
We use it to measure land, design buildings, build bridges, and even make art. Geometry is everywhere — in both flat surfaces (2D) and solid objects (3D).

## Perimeter of Shapes

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains perimeter as the distance around a shape.  
Prompt: If you walk all the way around a rectangle, what are you measuring?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

The **perimeter** is the total distance around a shape.  
- Rectangle: `P = 2(length + width)`  
- Triangle: `P = a + b + c`  
- Square: `P = 4 × side`  
Perimeter is useful for fencing a yard, framing a picture, or outlining any boundary.

## Area of Shapes

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces area as space inside a shape.  
Prompt: How is area different from perimeter?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

The **area** is how much space a shape covers inside its boundary.  
- Rectangle: `A = length × width`  
- Triangle: `A = ½ × base × height`  
- Circle: `A = πr²`  
Area helps us figure out things like how much paint covers a wall or how much grass grows in a field.

## Understanding Angles

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explores types of angles.  
Prompt: What’s the difference between an acute angle and an obtuse angle?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

An **angle** is formed when two rays meet at a point.  
- Acute angle: less than 90°  
- Right angle: exactly 90°  
- Obtuse angle: between 90° and 180°  
- Straight angle: 180°  
Angles are key in construction, art, and navigation.

## Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects perimeter, area, and angles to everyday life.  
Prompt: Where have you used perimeter, area, or angles outside of school?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Perimeter: measuring the border for a fence.  
- Area: calculating carpet or floor space.  
- Angles: cutting wood, tiling, or aiming in sports.  

Geometry is a practical tool for problem-solving in the real world.
