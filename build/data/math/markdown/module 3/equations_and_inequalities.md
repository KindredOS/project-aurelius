## What Are Equations?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces equations as statements of equality.  
Prompt: What does the equal sign really mean in a math problem?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

An **equation** is a mathematical statement that says two expressions are equal.  
- Example: `3 + 5 = 8` shows both sides are the same value.  
- In algebra, equations often include variables, like `x + 4 = 10`.  
Solving an equation means finding the value of the variable that makes both sides equal.

## Solving One-Step Equations

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how to solve simple equations with one operation.  
Prompt: How do you undo addition or multiplication to find the variable?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

To solve `x + 7 = 12`, subtract 7 from both sides to get `x = 5`.  
To solve `3x = 18`, divide both sides by 3 to get `x = 6`.  
The key idea: whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other.

## What Are Inequalities?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces inequalities as comparisons.  
Prompt: How is `x < 10` different from `x = 10`?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

An **inequality** shows that one value is greater than, less than, or sometimes equal to another.  
- Symbols: `<` (less than), `>` (greater than), `≤` (less than or equal to), `≥` (greater than or equal to).  
- Example: `x < 10` means x can be any number less than 10, not just one solution.

## Solving Inequalities

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how solving inequalities is like solving equations, with one twist.  
Prompt: What happens when you multiply or divide an inequality by a negative number?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

To solve `x + 4 < 9`, subtract 4 to get `x < 5`.  
When multiplying or dividing both sides by a **negative number**, you must **flip the inequality sign**.  
- Example: `-2x > 6` becomes `x < -3`.  
This rule keeps the inequality true.

## Graphing Equations and Inequalities

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects algebra to visual models.  
Prompt: How can a graph show all the solutions to an inequality?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Equations have specific solutions (points), while inequalities have many solutions (regions).  
- On a number line, `x = 4` is a point.  
- On a number line, `x > 4` is a shaded ray starting at 4.  
Graphing helps us see the difference between one solution and many.

## Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows why equations and inequalities matter in daily life.  
Prompt: When might you use an equation or inequality in real life?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Using an equation to figure out how many tickets you can buy with a set amount of money.  
- Using an inequality to budget: “I need to spend **less than** $50.”  
Equations and inequalities help us model problems and make decisions.
