## What Are Exponents?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces exponents as repeated multiplication.  
Prompt: How is 2³ different from 2 × 3?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

An **exponent** tells how many times to multiply a number by itself.  
- Example: 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8  
- Example: 5² = 5 × 5 = 25  
The small raised number (the exponent) shows how many times the base is multiplied. Exponents are a shortcut for repeated multiplication.

## Properties of Exponents

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains the rules that exponents follow.  
Prompt: What happens if you multiply numbers with the same base?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Exponents follow predictable rules:  
- `aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ`  
- `(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ`  
- `a⁰ = 1` (for any a ≠ 0)  
These properties help simplify expressions and make calculations easier.

## What Are Roots?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces roots as the opposite of exponents.  
Prompt: What number times itself equals 25?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **square root** asks what number multiplied by itself equals the given number.  
- √25 = 5 because 5 × 5 = 25  
- √81 = 9 because 9 × 9 = 81  

Roots are the inverse of exponents. Just like subtraction “undoes” addition, roots “undo” powers.

## Cube Roots and Beyond

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section expands roots to higher powers.  
Prompt: What number times itself three times equals 27?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **cube root** finds the number that multiplies by itself three times to make a given number.  
- ∛27 = 3 because 3 × 3 × 3 = 27  

Roots can extend to fourth, fifth, or higher orders. Each matches the reverse of an exponent.

## Exponents, Roots, and Real Life

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects powers and roots to practical contexts.  
Prompt: Where do you see exponents or roots in real life?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Exponents show up in science (powers of 10 in chemistry or physics).  
- Roots appear in geometry (finding the side of a square from its area).  
- Growth and decay (interest rates, population growth, or half-life).  

Exponents and roots help describe how numbers grow, shrink, or balance in everyday situations.
