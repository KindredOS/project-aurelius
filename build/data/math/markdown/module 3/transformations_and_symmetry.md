## What Are Transformations?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces transformations as ways shapes move or change position.  
Prompt: How can you move a shape without changing what it is?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **transformation** is a change in position, size, or orientation of a shape. The shape itself stays the same, but the way it looks on the plane changes.  
The four main transformations are: **translations, rotations, reflections, and dilations**.

## Translations (Slides)

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains translations as slides.  
Prompt: If you slide a triangle 3 units to the right, how does it change?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **translation** slides a shape without turning it or resizing it.  
- Every point moves the same distance in the same direction.  
- The shape stays congruent (exactly the same size and shape).

## Rotations (Turns)

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces rotations as turns around a point.  
Prompt: What happens if you rotate a square 90° around its center?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **rotation** turns a shape around a fixed point.  
- The amount of turn is measured in degrees.  
- Common rotations are 90°, 180°, and 270°.  
The shape keeps its size and angles.

## Reflections (Flips)

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explores reflections as mirror images.  
Prompt: What do you notice when you flip a shape over a line?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **reflection** flips a shape across a line (the “mirror line”).  
- The reflected shape is the same size but reversed.  
- Each point is the same distance from the line as its mirror image.

## Dilations (Resizing)

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces dilations as enlarging or shrinking.  
Prompt: How does doubling the size of a square change its area?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **dilation** makes a shape larger or smaller but keeps its proportions the same.  
- The scale factor tells how much bigger or smaller the shape gets.  
- Example: A scale factor of 2 doubles the size of each side.

## Understanding Symmetry

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains symmetry as balance in shapes.  
Prompt: What makes a butterfly symmetrical?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A shape has **symmetry** if it can be divided into matching parts.  
- **Line symmetry**: one half is a mirror image of the other.  
- **Rotational symmetry**: the shape looks the same after a rotation.  

Symmetry is common in nature, design, and art.

## Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects transformations and symmetry to everyday life.  
Prompt: Where do you see symmetry or transformations outside of math class?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Translations: sliding puzzle pieces.  
- Rotations: turning gears.  
- Reflections: mirror images or logos.  
- Dilations: resizing images on a computer.  
- Symmetry: architecture, butterflies, and snowflakes.  

Transformations and symmetry help us understand patterns and design in the real world.
