## Reading Change on Graphs

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces graphs as a way to see change.  
Prompt: How can you tell if something is increasing or decreasing just by looking at a graph?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Graphs let us see patterns of growth and decline.  
- An **upward slope** means the function is increasing.  
- A **downward slope** means it is decreasing.  
- A **flat slope** means no change.  

Visual interpretation connects algebraic rules with real-world meaning.

## Slopes and Derivatives

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section links the slope of a tangent line to instantaneous change.  
Prompt: What does the steepness of a graph tell us about how fast something is changing?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

The **derivative** at a point is the slope of the tangent line there.  
- Steeper slope → faster rate of change.  
- Positive slope → increasing.  
- Negative slope → decreasing.  

Graphs make derivatives easier to understand.

## Areas and Integrals

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how area under a curve represents accumulation.  
Prompt: What does the shaded area under a speed-time graph represent?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

The **integral** represents total accumulation.  
- In a speed-time graph, the area = total distance traveled.  
- In a growth graph, the area = total increase over time.  

Integrals turn visual areas into meaningful totals.

## Connecting Derivatives and Integrals

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how change and accumulation are linked.  
Prompt: How can the slope of a graph and the area under it describe the same system?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Derivatives and integrals are connected:  
- The derivative measures **instantaneous change**.  
- The integral measures **total accumulation**.  

Together, they describe both the moment and the whole process.

## Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section highlights how interpreting graphs helps us in real life.  
Prompt: Where have you seen graphs used to explain change outside of math class?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Business: profit vs. cost curves.  
- Science: graphs of speed, population, or energy use.  
- Medicine: heart rate or drug dosage graphs.  
- Everyday life: charts of sleep, exercise, or spending.  

Graphical interpretation helps us understand and explain real-world change.
