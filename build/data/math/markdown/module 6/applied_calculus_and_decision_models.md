## What Is Applied Calculus?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces applied calculus as using derivatives and integrals to solve real problems.

**Applied calculus** takes abstract ideas like derivatives (rates of change) and integrals (accumulations) and uses them to model decisions in business, science, and everyday life. It transforms theory into practical tools.

## Optimization in Decision Making

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how calculus finds maximums and minimums.  
Prompt: How could you use math to find the point of maximum profit?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Derivatives identify where functions increase or decrease.  
- Critical points (where slope = 0) show possible peaks or valleys.  
- These methods help maximize profit, minimize cost, or optimize efficiency.  

Optimization is one of the most powerful applications of calculus.

## Modeling Change Over Time

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how calculus tracks dynamic systems.  
Prompt: How can math describe population growth that speeds up and then slows down?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Derivatives model how quickly growth changes.  
- Integrals measure the total population over time.  
- Together, they show both the rate and the accumulated result.  

This is essential in biology, economics, and environmental science.

## Calculus in Probability and Risk

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects calculus to probability models.  
Prompt: How might a business use math to plan for uncertain outcomes?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Integrals calculate probabilities over continuous ranges.  
- Expected value uses calculus to weigh risks and rewards.  
- These tools support decisions in finance, insurance, and engineering.  

Applied calculus helps us manage uncertainty.
