### Rates and Ratios

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how ratios describe health trends.  
Prompt: What does it mean if 3 out of every 100 people in a town get the flu this year?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Health data often use ratios and percentages:  
- **Incidence rate**: new cases ÷ population.  
- **Mortality rate**: deaths ÷ population.  
- **Vaccination coverage**: % of people immunized.  

Ratios make health risks easy to compare.

### Modeling Disease Spread

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces mathematical models of epidemics.  
Prompt: How can math help predict how fast a disease might spread?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Models like the **SIR model** divide populations into groups:  
- S = Susceptible  
- I = Infected  
- R = Recovered  

Equations estimate how infections grow or shrink over time, guiding prevention efforts.

### Policy and Decision Making

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects math to government choices.  
Prompt: Why do leaders need numbers before making public health rules?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Math informs decisions like:  
- When to open or close schools.

Policies rely on evidence to balance safety, cost, and fairness.
