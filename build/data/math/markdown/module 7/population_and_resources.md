## Population Growth and Resource Allocation

---

### Modeling Population Growth

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces exponential and logistic models of population.  
Prompt: How is a population that doubles every 20 years different from one that levels off over time?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Populations can grow rapidly, often modeled with **exponential functions**. But resources place limits, leading to **logistic growth**, where growth slows and stabilizes. These models show the balance between growth and limits.

### Carrying Capacity

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains carrying capacity as a natural limit.  
Prompt: Why can’t a population grow forever without limits?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Carrying capacity** is the maximum population size an environment can support.  
- Above this limit, resources run out.  
- Logistic growth models this balance.  

Carrying capacity depends on food, water, land, and energy.

### Resource Allocation

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects population growth to distribution of resources.  
Prompt: How might math help decide how much food or water each person gets in a community?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Resource allocation uses ratios, proportions, and optimization.  
- Example: dividing water equally among households.  
- Example: budgeting energy use between industries.  

Math helps distribute limited supplies fairly and efficiently.

### Inequality in Resources

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section highlights unequal distribution.  
Prompt: Why might two countries with the same population have very different standards of living?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Not all resources are shared equally.  
- Some groups consume more than others.  
- Metrics like **per capita consumption** and **Gini coefficients** measure inequality.  

Math makes inequities visible so they can be addressed.

### Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects population and resource models to global issues.  
Prompt: Where do you see math being used to plan for population growth?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Governments project population to plan schools, hospitals, and housing.  
- Farmers estimate crop yields to feed communities.  
- Scientists model resource use for sustainability.  
- NGOs allocate aid in crisis zones.  

Population and resource math supports planning for a sustainable future.
