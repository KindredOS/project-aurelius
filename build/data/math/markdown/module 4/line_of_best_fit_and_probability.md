## What Is a Line of Best Fit?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces best-fit lines as models for data trends.  
Prompt: If points on a scatter plot don’t line up perfectly, how can we still describe the pattern?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **line of best fit** is a straight line drawn through data on a scatter plot to show the overall trend. It doesn’t pass through every point but represents the relationship as closely as possible.  

The line helps us see whether the relationship is positive, negative, or neutral.

## Drawing and Interpreting Best-Fit Lines

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how to interpret a best-fit line.  
Prompt: What does it mean if the line goes upward from left to right?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- An **upward slope** means as x increases, y increases (positive correlation).  
- A **downward slope** means as x increases, y decreases (negative correlation).  
- A **flat slope** means little to no relationship.  

The slope and intercept of the line tell the story of the data.

## Using Best-Fit Lines for Predictions

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how best-fit lines help us make predictions.  
Prompt: How could you use a line of best fit to guess a student’s test score after 5 hours of study?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Once we have a line of best fit, we can plug in new x-values to predict y-values.  
Example: If the best-fit line is y = 10x + 50, then 5 hours of study (x = 5) predicts a score of 100.  

This is called **interpolation** when inside the data range, and **extrapolation** when beyond it.

## Introduction to Probability

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces probability as the mathematics of chance.  
Prompt: If you flip a coin, what’s the probability of heads?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Probability** measures the likelihood of an event happening.  
- Probability = (Number of favorable outcomes) ÷ (Total possible outcomes).  
- Example: A coin has 1 chance of heads out of 2 possible outcomes → 1/2.  

Probability connects chance events to numbers between 0 and 1.

## Probability Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects probability to data.  
Prompt: How can we use probability to predict outcomes over time?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Probability models describe the likelihood of events:  
- A fair die: each number has a 1/6 chance.  
- Weather forecasts: 70% chance of rain means it should rain on 7 out of 10 similar days.  
Probability models combine math with data to help make informed decisions.
