## Sleep, Rest, and Circadian Health

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces the concept of sleep and circadian rhythms. Prompt: Why do humans need sleep, and what happens if we don’t get enough?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Sleep is essential for our bodies and minds. It helps repair tissues, store memories, and balance hormones. Without enough sleep, we feel tired, moody, and have trouble focusing. Understanding how sleep works helps us take care of our health.

## What is a Circadian Rhythm?

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains your body’s internal clock. Prompt: How does your body know when to wake up and when to sleep?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Your circadian rhythm is a 24-hour cycle that helps regulate sleep, alertness, and energy levels. It responds to light and darkness, telling your body when it’s time to wake up or wind down. Healthy rhythms help you feel more awake during the day and restful at night.

## Sleep Hygiene Basics

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section covers habits for better sleep. Prompt: What are some things you can do to improve your sleep at night?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Sleep hygiene means creating good habits that lead to better sleep. That includes going to bed and waking up at the same time, limiting screens before bed, keeping your room cool and dark, and avoiding caffeine late in the day.

## Stages of Sleep

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section describes the different stages of sleep. Prompt: What happens during the different parts of your sleep cycle?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Sleep has several stages, including light sleep, deep sleep, and REM (rapid eye movement) sleep. Each stage helps your body and brain recover in different ways. Deep sleep restores your body, while REM sleep supports memory and learning.

## Rest vs. Sleep

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section highlights the difference between resting and sleeping. Prompt: How is rest different from sleep, and why do we need both?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Rest means giving your body or mind a break, but you’re still awake. It can include quiet time, reading, or meditation. Sleep is deeper and helps the body repair itself. Both are important for energy, focus, and well-being.

## Common Sleep Challenges

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section looks at sleep problems students might face. Prompt: What are some reasons you might not sleep well, and how can you fix them?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Things like stress, too much screen time, noisy environments, or an irregular schedule can affect sleep. Creating a calming bedtime routine and being consistent can help your brain know it’s time to rest.

## Naps and Energy Breaks

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section discusses the role of naps and short breaks. Prompt: When might a short nap or rest break be helpful during the day?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

A quick nap or a short rest can boost energy and focus, especially if you didn’t sleep well the night before. Naps should be short—about 20 minutes—so they don’t interfere with nighttime sleep.

\[interactive element]

## Creating a Sleep-Friendly Routine

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section encourages students to design a healthy sleep routine. Prompt: What are three steps you can take tonight to sleep better?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

A sleep routine helps train your body to expect rest. You might dim the lights, turn off screens an hour before bed, write in a journal, or listen to calm music. Doing the same things each night signals to your body that it’s time to sleep.
