## Systems Thinking

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces the concept of systems and why understanding how parts work together is essential in technology.
**Prompt:** Can you think of something in your life that works because multiple parts work together?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Systems thinking is a way of seeing how parts connect to form a whole. Whether it’s a car engine, a school, or a computer network, every system depends on the coordination of its parts to function properly.

## What is a System?

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section defines systems as organized sets of parts working toward a goal.
**Prompt:** How would you describe your school or home as a system?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

A system is made of inputs, processes, and outputs. Each part has a role, and the success of the whole depends on how well those parts work together. Systems can be mechanical, biological, social, or digital.

## Inputs, Processes, Outputs

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores how systems take in resources, do work, and produce results.
**Prompt:** What are the inputs and outputs of a computer or a kitchen?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Every system starts with inputs—like energy, information, or materials. These inputs are processed in some way, and the system produces an output. Recognizing this pattern helps us understand and improve systems.

## Feedback Loops

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains how systems adapt or self-correct through feedback.
**Prompt:** What’s an example of a system adjusting based on results or data?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Feedback loops help systems stay balanced. For example, a thermostat adjusts the temperature based on readings. Positive feedback amplifies change, while negative feedback stabilizes a system.

\[interactive element]

## Open vs. Closed Systems

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section compares systems that interact with their environment to those that don’t.
**Prompt:** Is your body an open system or a closed one? Why?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Open systems exchange energy and information with their surroundings, like ecosystems or businesses. Closed systems are more isolated. Understanding the difference helps in designing efficient and resilient technologies.

## Complex and Emergent Behavior

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section shows how simple rules can create complex outcomes in systems.
**Prompt:** What’s a system where small changes lead to big effects?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

In complex systems, interactions between parts can lead to unexpected results. These emergent behaviors can be seen in weather patterns, traffic flow, and even group decision-making.

## Systems Mapping and Diagrams

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces visual tools to analyze and improve systems.
**Prompt:** Can you draw or explain how a vending machine or website works as a system?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Diagrams like flowcharts, system maps, and network graphs help us visualize how parts connect. They’re used in engineering, business, and design to plan improvements or solve problems.

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section connects systems thinking to careers and everyday problem-solving.
