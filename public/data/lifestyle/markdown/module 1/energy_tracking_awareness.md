## Energy Tracking and Awareness

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces the idea of personal energy awareness. Prompt: How do your energy levels change during the day?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Your body and mind go through cycles. You might feel full of energy in the morning and tired later. Noticing these patterns helps you plan your day better. Paying attention to when you feel your best helps you make smart choices.

## What Affects Energy Levels?

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores factors that impact your energy. Prompt: What things can cause your energy to rise or fall?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Energy levels change because of things like sleep, food, exercise, stress, and even your environment. Knowing what lifts you up or drains you helps you take charge of your energy.

## Using Energy Trackers

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains how to use tools to track energy. Prompt: How could keeping track of your energy help you?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Energy trackers help you see patterns. You can use a chart, an app, or a simple journal to record when you feel tired or focused. Over time, you’ll see what habits make you feel your best.

## Energy and Emotions

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section shows how feelings and energy connect. Prompt: How do your emotions affect your energy levels?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Strong emotions can raise or lower your energy. Feeling excited might give you a boost, while worry can wear you out. Being aware of these connections helps you care for both your feelings and your energy.

## Peaks and Slumps

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section describes common times of high and low energy. Prompt: When do you usually feel your most awake or most tired?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Most people have energy highs and lows during the day. You might feel sharp in the morning, sleepy after lunch, and focused again in the evening. Tracking these rhythms can help you know the best times for work or rest.

## Matching Tasks to Energy

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section helps students align tasks with energy. Prompt: What are some things you do better when you have more energy?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

When you have lots of energy, it’s a good time to do harder tasks like homework or physical activities. When your energy is low, you might do better with simple chores, reading, or taking a break. Matching tasks to energy helps you succeed.
