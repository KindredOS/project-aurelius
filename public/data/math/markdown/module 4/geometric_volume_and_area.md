## What Is Volume?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces volume as the space inside a 3D object.  
Prompt: How is measuring the inside of a box different from measuring around it?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Volume** measures how much space a solid object takes up.  
- Example: A box with dimensions 3 × 4 × 5 has a volume of 60 cubic units.  
Volume is always measured in cubic units (cm³, m³, in³).  

It tells us how much a container can hold—like water in a tank or cereal in a box.

## Volume Formulas

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows formulas for common 3D shapes.  
Prompt: What’s the formula for the volume of a cylinder?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- **Rectangular prism (box):** V = length × width × height  
- **Cube:** V = side³  
- **Cylinder:** V = πr²h  
- **Cone:** V = ⅓πr²h  
- **Sphere:** V = ⁴⁄₃πr³  

Each formula matches the way space is filled inside the shape.

## What Is Surface Area?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces surface area as the “skin” of a 3D object.  
Prompt: How is surface area like wrapping a gift box?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Surface area** is the total area of all the outside faces of a 3D object.  
- Rectangular prism: add the areas of all six rectangles.  
- Cylinder: 2πr² (top and bottom) + 2πrh (side).  

Surface area measures covering, like wrapping paper or paint.

## Surface Area Formulas

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section lists key formulas.  
Prompt: How would you find the surface area of a sphere?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- **Cube:** SA = 6s²  
- **Rectangular prism:** SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh  
- **Cylinder:** SA = 2πr² + 2πrh  
- **Sphere:** SA = 4πr²  
- **Cone:** SA = πr² + πrl  

Each part of the formula represents one face or surface of the shape.

## Comparing Volume and Surface Area

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section helps students see the difference.  
Prompt: How can two shapes have the same volume but different surface areas?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Volume measures how much space is inside.  
- Surface area measures how much material covers the outside.  

Example: Two different boxes can both hold 60 cubic units, but one might need more wrapping paper.

## Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects volume and surface area to the real world.  
Prompt: Where do you use volume or surface area in everyday life?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Volume: filling a swimming pool, measuring drinks, packaging food.  
- Surface area: painting walls, designing packaging, solar panel coverage.  

Understanding both helps in construction, design, and science.
