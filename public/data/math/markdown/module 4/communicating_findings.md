Prompt: Why is it important to explain your math results to others?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

## Telling the Story Behind the Data

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how math results connect to real meaning.  
Prompt: What’s the difference between saying “the average is 75” and “most students scored about 75 on the test”?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Math should tell a story, not just give a number.  
- Raw numbers = information.  
- Interpreted numbers = insight.  

Adding context helps people understand the “why” and “so what” of your results.

## Using Graphs and Visuals

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how visuals make results clearer.  
Prompt: How does a graph sometimes explain data better than a paragraph?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Charts, graphs, and diagrams make patterns visible.  
- Line graphs show trends over time.  
- Bar graphs compare categories.  
- Scatter plots show relationships.  

Choosing the right visual makes your findings easier to understand.

## Clear Explanations and Language

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section focuses on using simple, precise language.  
Prompt: How can you explain math so someone without a math background understands it?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Avoid jargon and break down terms into plain language.  
- Instead of “standard deviation,” say “how spread out the data is.”  
- Instead of “linear correlation,” say “as one goes up, the other goes up too.”  

Clarity builds trust and understanding.

## Supporting Claims With Evidence

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains why evidence matters.  
Prompt: How does showing your work make your conclusion stronger?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

When you share results, back them up with numbers, steps, or visuals.  
- Example: “Our sales increased by 20% this year” is stronger with a graph showing the growth.  
Evidence proves your claim and makes it more convincing.
