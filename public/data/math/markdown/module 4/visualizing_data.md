This section introduces the purpose of graphs and charts.

[Prompt Wrap End]

**Visualizing data** helps us see patterns, trends, and comparisons at a glance. While numbers in a table may be hard to interpret, a chart or graph can make the story clear and accessible.

## Bar Graphs and Histograms

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains bar graphs and histograms.  
Prompt: How could you show the favorite sports of everyone in class?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- **Bar Graphs**: use bars to compare categories (e.g., basketball, soccer, baseball).  
- **Histograms**: use bars to show how numbers fall into ranges (e.g., ages 10–12, 13–15).  
Both are great for showing frequency and distribution.

## Line Graphs

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces line graphs for continuous data.  
Prompt: How would you show the temperature outside over one week?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **line graph** connects data points to show change over time.  
- Rising lines mean increase.  
- Falling lines mean decrease.  
Line graphs are ideal for trends, such as growth, decline, or cycles.

## Scatter Plots

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces scatter plots to show relationships.  
Prompt: How could you show the connection between hours studied and test scores?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **scatter plot** places points on a graph to see how two variables are related.  
- A positive trend goes upward (more study → higher score).  
- A negative trend goes downward (more speed → less time).  
Scatter plots help us explore correlations.

## Pie Charts

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains pie charts as parts of a whole.  
Prompt: How could you show the percentage of time spent on homework, sports, and fun?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **pie chart** divides a circle into slices to show proportions. Each slice represents a fraction or percentage of the total. Pie charts are great for seeing how parts add up to 100%.

## Choosing the Right Visualization

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section teaches how to pick the best graph.  
Prompt: How do you decide which type of graph to use?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Bar graphs → compare categories.  
- Histograms → show ranges.  
- Line graphs → show change over time.  
- Scatter plots → show relationships.  
- Pie charts → show proportions of a whole.  

The right choice makes the data clearer.

## Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects data visualization to real life.  
Prompt: Where do you see graphs and charts outside of school?  
[Prompt Wrap End]
