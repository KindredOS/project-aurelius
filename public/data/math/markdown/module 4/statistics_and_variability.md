## Measures of Center

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains mean, median, and mode.  
Prompt: What’s one way to find the “typical” value in a data set?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- **Mean**: add all values, then divide by how many there are.  
- **Median**: the middle value when data is ordered.  
- **Mode**: the value that appears most often.  

These measures give us different ways to describe the “center” of data.

## Measures of Variability

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces range, interquartile range, and standard deviation.  
Prompt: How can you tell if numbers are close together or spread out?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- **Range**: biggest value – smallest value.  
- **Interquartile Range (IQR)**: spread of the middle 50% of data.  
- **Standard Deviation**: average distance of values from the mean.  

Variability tells us how consistent or unpredictable data is.

## Understanding Distributions

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explores how data can be shaped.  
Prompt: What does a “bell curve” show about data?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Data can be **symmetrical, skewed, or uniform**.  
- A **bell curve** (normal distribution) shows most values close to the mean.  
- A **skewed distribution** leans to one side.  
Recognizing distributions helps us interpret results more accurately.

## Outliers and Their Effect

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains why unusual values matter.  
Prompt: How does one really big or really small number change the average?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

An **outlier** is a value that’s much higher or lower than the rest.  
- Outliers can raise or lower the mean a lot.  
- They usually don’t affect the median as much.  
Understanding outliers helps us avoid misleading conclusions.

## Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects statistics and variability to real-world uses.  
Prompt: Where do you see people using statistics outside of math class?  
[Prompt Wrap End]
