## What Are Quadratic Models?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces quadratic functions as curved models.  
Prompt: How is the graph of y = x² different from y = 2x + 3?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **quadratic model** is an equation where the highest exponent is 2, like y = ax² + bx + c.  
- The graph is a parabola (U-shaped curve).  
- Parabolas can open upward or downward depending on the coefficient a.  

Quadratic models are used for motion, area, and other problems where change is curved.

## Polynomial Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces polynomials of higher degree.  
Prompt: What makes y = x³ different from y = x²?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Polynomials** are functions with terms that have whole-number exponents.  
- Examples: y = x³ – 4x, y = 2x⁴ + x² – 1.  
- Degree tells us the highest exponent.  

Cubic, quartic, and higher-degree polynomials create more complex curves that can model turning points and real-world patterns.

## Rational Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces rational functions as ratios.  
Prompt: What happens when x = 0 in y = 1/x?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **rational function** is a fraction where the numerator and denominator are polynomials.  
- Example: y = (x² – 1) / (x + 2).  
- They often have **asymptotes** (lines the graph approaches but never touches).  

Rational models describe rates, proportions, and situations where growth or limits matter.

## Comparing the Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section compares quadratic, polynomial, and rational functions.  
Prompt: Why would one type of model work better than another?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- **Quadratics**: simple curves, one turning point.  
- **Polynomials**: more complex curves, multiple turning points.  
- **Rational functions**: involve division, with restrictions and asymptotes.  

Choosing the right model depends on the situation being studied.
