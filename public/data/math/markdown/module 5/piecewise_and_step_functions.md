## What Are Piecewise Functions?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces piecewise functions as “rules that change.”  
Prompt: How can a function follow different rules depending on the input?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **piecewise function** is made of two or more rules, each applying to a different part of the domain.  
- Example: A taxi fare might be \$3 to start, plus \$2 per mile after.  
- One rule applies for the flat fee, another for distance.  

Piecewise functions let us model situations where conditions change.

## Writing Piecewise Functions

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how to write a function with multiple parts.  
Prompt: How would you write a function that costs \$5 when x < 10, and \$10 when x ≥ 10?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Piecewise functions are written with braces { } to show different cases:  

f(x) = { 5   if x < 10  
         10  if x ≥ 10 }  

Each “piece” is tied to a specific condition.

## Graphing Piecewise Functions

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how to draw graphs for piecewise rules.  
Prompt: What would the graph look like if one rule is a line and another is flat?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

On a graph, piecewise functions look like different segments stitched together.  
- Some pieces may be lines, others may be curves.  
- Open and closed circles show where rules switch.  

Graphs help us see the full story of changing conditions.

## What Are Step Functions?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces step functions as “stair-step” graphs.  
Prompt: How could a cell phone plan that charges by the gigabyte look on a graph?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **step function** increases or decreases in jumps, not smoothly.  
- Example: A phone plan charges \$10 for each GB of data.  
- The cost jumps in steps (10, 20, 30…) instead of rising continuously.  

Step functions are a special type of piecewise function.

## Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects piecewise and step functions to real life.  
Prompt: Where do you see rules that change depending on the situation?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Postage costs based on weight brackets.  
- Movie ticket prices by age group.  
- Tax brackets in income taxes.  
- Subscription plans with usage tiers.  

Piecewise and step functions model how costs and rules shift in everyday life.
