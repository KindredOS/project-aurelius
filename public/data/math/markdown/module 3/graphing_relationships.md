## What Does a Graph Show?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces graphs as pictures of relationships.  
Prompt: How does a graph help us see how two things are connected?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **graph** is a visual way to show how numbers relate. It helps us see patterns, trends, and comparisons.  
- The horizontal axis (x-axis) usually shows the input or independent variable.  
- The vertical axis (y-axis) usually shows the output or dependent variable.  
Graphs let us “see” math relationships instead of just reading them in symbols.

## Plotting Points

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains the basics of coordinate graphs.  
Prompt: How do you find where to put the point (3, 2) on a graph?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A point is written as an **ordered pair (x, y)**.  
- The first number tells how far to move along the x-axis.  
- The second number tells how far to move along the y-axis.  
Example: (3, 2) is 3 steps right and 2 steps up. Points are the building blocks of graphs.

## Graphing Linear Relationships

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how equations turn into lines.  
Prompt: What does the equation y = 2x + 1 look like when graphed?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A linear equation like `y = 2x + 1` makes a straight line.  
- The number 2 is the **slope**, showing the steepness of the line.  
- The +1 is the **y-intercept**, where the line crosses the y-axis.  
Graphing linear equations shows how one variable changes at a constant rate with the other.

## Graphing Nonlinear Relationships

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces curves on a graph.  
Prompt: How is a parabola different from a straight line?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Not all relationships are straight lines.  
- `y = x²` makes a **parabola** (U-shaped curve).  
- `y = √x` makes a curve that rises more slowly.  
- Exponential functions like `y = 2^x` rise very quickly.  
These nonlinear graphs show change that is not constant.

## Interpreting Graphs

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section teaches students to read meaning from graphs.  
Prompt: How can a graph tell you whether something is increasing, decreasing, or staying the same?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Graphs tell stories:  
- An upward slope means growth.  
- A downward slope means decrease.  
- A flat line means no change.  
Looking at the shape of a graph helps us understand real-world situations quickly.

## Real-Life Connections

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects graphs to everyday life.  
Prompt: Where do you see graphs being used outside of math class?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Graphs appear in:  
- Weather forecasts (temperature changes over time).  
- Sports stats (points scored per game).  
- Business reports (profits and losses).  
- Science experiments (how one variable affects another).  

Graphing is a tool that turns data into insight.
