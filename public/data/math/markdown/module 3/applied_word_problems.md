## What Are Applied Word Problems?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces word problems as real-life scenarios that can be modeled with math.  
Prompt: Why do you think math problems are sometimes written as stories instead of just numbers?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Applied word problems** take everyday situations and turn them into math challenges. They help us connect math to real life, like planning a budget, figuring out travel time, or designing a space. The goal is to translate words into numbers, equations, or diagrams to solve problems.

## Translating Words Into Equations

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how to turn sentences into algebra.  
Prompt: How can you write “three more than twice a number is eleven” as an equation?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Word problems often hide equations in plain language.  
- “Three more than twice a number is eleven” → `2x + 3 = 11`.  
- “The total cost is 5 dollars per ticket times the number of tickets” → `C = 5t`.  

Learning to translate makes solving much easier.

## Geometry in Word Problems

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how geometry can appear in applied problems.  
Prompt: If a garden is 6 meters long and 4 meters wide, how can you find the area?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Geometry word problems ask us to measure, compare, or build with shapes.  
- Perimeter: fencing a yard.  
- Area: painting a wall.  
- Volume: filling a box with soil or water.  

These problems show how geometry helps us in construction, design, and planning.

## Multi-Step Problems

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces problems that require more than one step.  
Prompt: If a taxi charges \$3 to start and \$2 per mile, how would you find the cost for 5 miles?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Multi-step problems combine operations.  
- Example: \$3 + 2 × 5 = \$13.  
They often mix addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, so planning the order of steps is important.

## Strategies for Solving

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section gives students tools to approach challenging problems.  
Prompt: What can you do first if a word problem feels overwhelming?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Good strategies include:  
- Read the problem carefully.  
- Underline important numbers and words.  
- Draw a diagram or chart.  
- Write an equation that matches the story.  
- Check if the solution makes sense.  

These steps build confidence and accuracy.

## Real-Life Connections

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how word problem skills are practical.  
Prompt: Can you think of a time when you solved a real problem using math?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Word problems mirror real decision-making:  
- Planning expenses when shopping.  
- Estimating travel times.  
- Dividing chores or splitting bills.  
- Figuring out how much paint, food, or material is needed.  

Applied word problems train us to use math as a life skill.
