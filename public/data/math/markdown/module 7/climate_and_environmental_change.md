### Climate Data and Trends

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces climate data as measurable change over time.  
Prompt: How can graphs of temperature or CO₂ levels help us understand climate change?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Climate change is studied through data. Scientists measure temperature, carbon dioxide levels, sea ice extent, and more. Graphs show long-term trends, like rising global average temperatures or increasing greenhouse gases.

### Rates of Change in Climate

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects calculus concepts to environmental change.  
Prompt: Why does it matter if CO₂ levels rise faster this year than last year?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Rate of change** describes how quickly a climate variable shifts.  
- Slope of a line on a CO₂ graph = how fast levels rise.  
- Acceleration shows if change is speeding up.  

Calculating rates helps predict future climate conditions.

### Modeling Climate Systems

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains mathematical models of climate.  
Prompt: How could equations predict future sea levels?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Climate models use equations to simulate Earth systems.  
- Differential equations describe energy flow and heat transfer.  
- Exponential functions show greenhouse gas growth.  
- Probabilistic models forecast extreme weather.  

These models guide scientists and policymakers.

### Environmental Impact and Resources

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explores connections between math, environment, and resources.  
Prompt: How might math help decide how much land to protect for forests?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Math helps measure resource use:  
- Area calculations for deforestation.  
- Ratios of renewable vs. nonrenewable energy.  
- Optimization for sustainable land and water use.  

Environmental problems are resource allocation problems, solved with math.

### Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows where math supports climate action.  
Prompt: Where do you see people using numbers to talk about climate issues?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Governments track emissions targets.  
- Engineers model renewable energy output.  
- Businesses measure carbon footprints.  
- Communities use data to plan for floods or droughts.  

Mathematics provides the evidence for environmental solutions.
