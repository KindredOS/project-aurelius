### Mathematics in Economics

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces how math describes economic systems.  
Prompt: How can equations or graphs show how money moves through a system?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Economics uses math to track production, spending, and exchange.  
- Equations represent supply and demand.  
- Graphs show prices rising or falling.  
- Models predict how markets respond to change.  

Math reveals patterns behind financial decisions.

### Supply and Demand Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains the core of market behavior.  
Prompt: What happens to prices when demand goes up but supply stays the same?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Supply and demand are modeled with intersecting curves.  
- **Demand curve**: price vs. quantity buyers want.  
- **Supply curve**: price vs. quantity sellers provide.  
- The intersection shows **equilibrium price**.  

Shifts in curves explain shortages, surpluses, and price changes.

### Measuring Inequality

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces metrics for fairness.  
Prompt: How might math help us see if wealth is shared fairly in a country?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Economists use numbers to measure inequality:  
- **Gini coefficient**: 0 = perfect equality, 1 = total inequality.  
- **Lorenz curve**: shows share of wealth owned by different groups.  

These tools visualize how resources are divided.

### Growth vs. Distribution

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explores trade-offs between overall growth and fairness.  
Prompt: Can a country’s economy grow while many people remain poor?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Sometimes economies grow (higher GDP) but inequality rises. Math helps compare:  
- Total growth vs. distribution of benefits.  
- Averages vs. per-person or per-group outcomes.  

This analysis shapes policy debates about fairness.

### Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects economics and inequality metrics to daily life.  
Prompt: Where do you see math being used to talk about money, jobs, or fairness?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Governments design tax systems using equations.  
- Businesses forecast sales and wages.

Math makes economic systems visible, helping societies work toward fairness.
