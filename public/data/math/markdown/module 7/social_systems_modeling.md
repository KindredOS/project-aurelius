### What Are Social Systems?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces social systems as networks of people, groups, and rules.  
Prompt: How is a school, a city, or even a country like a system with connected parts?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **social system** is made of individuals, communities, and institutions interacting.  
Mathematics helps us understand these interactions, measure influence, and predict outcomes.

### Networks and Graph Theory

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how networks describe social connections.  
Prompt: How could a graph show who is friends with whom in a class?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Graphs use **nodes** (people) and **edges** (connections) to model social networks.  
- Example: A network map of friendships.  
- Example: Trade routes between countries.  

Graph theory reveals patterns of influence and power.

### Modeling Social Change

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces math for trends and shifts.  
Prompt: How can math show how opinions or behaviors spread in a community?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Equations and probabilities model how ideas spread, similar to disease models.  
- Adoption of technology.  
- Political movements.  
- Social media influence.  

Math predicts how fast and how widely change can spread.

### Geopolitical Systems

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects math to global relationships.  
Prompt: How might math help explain alliances, conflicts, or trade between nations?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Matrices model trade balances.  
- Game theory models conflicts and cooperation.  
- Statistics analyze inequality between nations.  

Geopolitical models use math to explain large-scale human systems.
