## Scenario Modeling and Strategic Thinking

---

### What Are Scenarios?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces scenarios as “what if” situations for the future.  
Prompt: How could math help us imagine different outcomes for the same problem?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **scenario** is a possible future shaped by assumptions and choices.  
Math lets us create models for each scenario, test outcomes, and compare strategies.

### Building Scenario Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how to model different paths.  
Prompt: If energy use keeps rising, how could we model both renewable and nonrenewable options?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Scenario models test different inputs and rules:  
- Example: carbon emissions if renewable energy grows vs. if fossil fuels continue.  
- Example: costs under high inflation vs. low inflation.  

Changing assumptions shows a range of outcomes.

### Strategic Thinking with Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces strategy as planning for different outcomes.  
Prompt: Why might leaders plan for both the best and worst cases?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Strategic thinking uses scenario models to prepare flexible responses.  
- Best-case, worst-case, and most-likely outcomes.  
- Balancing risk, resources, and priorities.  

Math-based strategies improve resilience in uncertain situations.

### Tools for Scenario Analysis

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section highlights methods for exploring possibilities.  
Prompt: How can graphs, simulations, or decision trees help compare scenarios?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- **Graphs**: visualize trends and comparisons.  
- **Simulations**: test many possibilities quickly.  
- **Decision trees**: map choices and consequences.  

These tools help explain complex strategies clearly.
