## What Is Base 10?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces the idea of our number system.  
Prompt: Why do you think we use groups of ten to count numbers?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

The **base 10 system** (decimal system) is the way we count and write numbers in everyday life. It uses the digits 0–9 and organizes numbers by place value. When we reach 10, we move to the next place: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, and so on. This structure makes it easier to work with large numbers using only ten digits.

## Place Value in Base 10

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how each digit has a value based on its position.  
Prompt: What does the “7” mean in the number 742?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Each digit in a number has a place value that shows how many ones, tens, hundreds, or thousands it represents.  
- In 742: the 7 is hundreds, the 4 is tens, and the 2 is ones.  
- This is called **place value** and it’s what makes base 10 so powerful for organizing numbers.

## Expanded Form

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how to break numbers apart.  
Prompt: How can you write the number 326 as a sum of its parts?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Expanded form means writing a number by showing the value of each digit.  
- Example: 326 = 300 + 20 + 6.  
This helps us see clearly what each digit stands for and connects numbers to place value.

## Counting Beyond 9

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains what happens when we “carry over.”  
Prompt: What number comes after 9? Why does the digit change?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

When we count beyond 9, we move into the next place value.  
- After 9 comes 10, which means 1 group of ten and 0 ones.  
- After 99 comes 100, which means 1 group of one hundred and 0 tens and ones.  
This pattern continues no matter how big the numbers get.

## Real-Life Base 10

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects base 10 to daily life.  
Prompt: Where do you see base 10 being used in the real world?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

We use base 10 all the time:  
- Counting money in dollars and cents.  
- Measuring in the metric system (centimeters, meters, kilometers).  
- Reading numbers on clocks, calendars, and addresses.  

Base 10 is everywhere, and knowing how it works helps us understand the world around us.
