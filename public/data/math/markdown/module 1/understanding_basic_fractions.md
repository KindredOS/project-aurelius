## What Are Fractions?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces fractions as parts of a whole.  
Prompt: How would you show half of a pizza if you didn’t eat the whole thing?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **fraction** is a way of showing a part of a whole. The number on top (the numerator) tells how many parts you have. The number on the bottom (the denominator) tells how many equal parts the whole is divided into. For example, 1/2 means 1 out of 2 equal parts.

## Fractions on a Number Line

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how fractions can be placed on a number line.  
Prompt: Where would you put 1/2 on a number line between 0 and 1?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Fractions aren’t just pieces of objects—they also live on the number line.  
- 1/2 is exactly halfway between 0 and 1.  
- 1/4 is one step of four equal parts between 0 and 1.  
Number lines help us see fractions as numbers.

## Equivalent Fractions

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains how fractions can look different but mean the same.  
Prompt: How is 1/2 the same as 2/4?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Two fractions are **equivalent** if they represent the same value.  
- 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8.  
Multiplying or dividing both the numerator and denominator by the same number gives an equivalent fraction. This shows how fractions connect.

## Comparing Fractions

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section teaches how to tell which fraction is larger or smaller.  
Prompt: Which is bigger—3/4 or 2/3? How do you know?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

To compare fractions, we look at their sizes. Sometimes we use common denominators to compare fairly. For example:  
- 3/4 = 9/12  
- 2/3 = 8/12  
Since 9/12 is greater than 8/12, 3/4 is larger.

## Fractions in Real Life

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects fractions to everyday experiences.  
Prompt: Where do you see or use fractions outside of school?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Fractions appear in daily life all the time:  
- Cutting a pizza or pie.  
- Measuring ingredients in cooking.  
- Telling time (like a quarter past).  
- Splitting money or sharing items.  

Fractions help us describe and work with parts of a whole in the real world.
