## Defining and Non-Defining Attributes

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section helps students understand the difference between important shape properties and details that don’t matter.  
Prompt: What makes a triangle a triangle no matter its color or size?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **defining attribute** is what makes a shape what it is.  
- A triangle must be **closed** and have **three straight sides**.  
- A square must have **four equal sides** and **four right angles**.  

**Non-defining attributes** are details that don’t change the shape: color, size, or orientation. A triangle turned upside down is still a triangle.

## Building and Drawing Shapes

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explores composing shapes from smaller ones.  
Prompt: How can you put two triangles together to make a square?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

We can **compose shapes** by joining smaller ones:  
- Two right triangles make a rectangle or square.  
- A rectangle and a triangle can make a house shape.  
- A cube can be built from smaller cubes.  

Drawing and building helps us see how simple shapes combine to create new ones.

## Two-Dimensional and Three-Dimensional Shapes

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains flat vs. solid shapes.  
Prompt: What is the difference between a square and a cube?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- **2D shapes** are flat, like rectangles, squares, trapezoids, triangles, half-circles, and quarter-circles.  
- **3D shapes** are solid, like cubes, rectangular prisms, cones, and cylinders.  

3D shapes have faces made of 2D shapes. For example, a cube has six square faces.

## Partitioning Shapes Into Equal Parts

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces splitting shapes into equal shares.  
Prompt: If you cut a rectangle into 4 equal parts, what do you call each part?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

When we split a shape into equal shares, we use special names:  
- 2 equal parts → halves  
- 4 equal parts → fourths or quarters  

The whole is made up of these equal parts. Splitting into more equal parts makes smaller pieces.

## Real-Life Connections

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how shape reasoning appears in everyday life.  
Prompt: Where do you see halves or quarters outside of math class?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Cutting a sandwich in half or quarters.  
- Folding paper into equal parts.  
- Slicing a pizza into halves or quarters.  
- Building with blocks to form larger structures.  

Reasoning with shapes helps us understand design, balance, and fairness in sharing.
