## What Are Patterns?

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces mathematical patterns. Prompt: Have you ever noticed a pattern in numbers, shapes, or nature?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

A pattern is something that repeats or follows a rule. You might see patterns in shapes, music, weather, or numbers. Recognizing patterns helps us predict what comes next and understand how things are organized.

## Patterns in Numbers

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores patterns in number sequences. Prompt: What comes next in this pattern—2, 4, 6, 8...?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Number patterns follow specific rules. Some go up by the same amount (like +2), others may double or alternate. Finding these rules helps you solve problems faster and understand how math is structured.

## Patterns in Shapes

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section looks at geometric patterns and visual repetition. Prompt: What shapes or designs have you seen that repeat?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Shapes often follow patterns too! Think of tiles on a floor or beads in a bracelet. Patterns in shapes can rotate, flip, or grow in size. Exploring these helps you understand geometry and symmetry.

## Using Patterns to Predict

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section shows how patterns help us make predictions. Prompt: How can finding a pattern help you guess the next number or shape?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

If you understand a pattern, you can guess what’s next. This is called prediction. It’s a big part of math and science, like forecasting the weather or solving equations.

## Patterns in Equations

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section links patterns to algebraic thinking. Prompt: Can you describe a rule for how the output changes in an equation like y = 3x?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Equations often create number patterns. In y = 3x, every time x goes up by 1, y goes up by 3. Spotting this rule helps you understand how math models change.

## Finding Structure

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores structure in math problems. Prompt: How does breaking a big problem into smaller parts help you solve it?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Structure means seeing how parts of a problem connect. You might group numbers, break apart shapes, or find repeating steps. This helps you organize your thinking and solve problems efficiently.

## Patterns in Nature

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section connects mathematical patterns to the natural world. Prompt: What patterns have you seen in plants, animals, or weather?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Nature is full of patterns—spirals in seashells, stripes on animals, cycles of the moon. Math helps us describe and understand these beautiful and useful patterns.

## Creating Your Own Patterns

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section encourages students to design patterns with rules. Prompt: Can you make your own pattern and explain its rule?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Making your own patterns is a fun way to practice math. Try drawing a shape pattern, writing a number sequence, or inventing a rhythm. Explain the rule and challenge someone else to continue it!
