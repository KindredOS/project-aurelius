## What Is Combinatorics?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces combinatorics as the math of counting and arrangements.  
Prompt: How many different ways can you arrange three books on a shelf?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Combinatorics** studies how to count and organize possibilities.  
- Arrangements of items (order matters).  
- Selections of items (order doesn’t matter).  
It helps us answer “how many ways?” questions.

## Permutations

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains permutations as ordered arrangements.  
Prompt: How many different 3-digit codes can you make with the numbers 1, 2, and 3?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **permutation** is an arrangement where order matters.  
- Example: 123, 132, 213, 231, 312, 321 → 6 permutations.  
- Formula: n! (n factorial) counts the ways to order n items.  

Permutations show how many unique sequences are possible.

## Combinations

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces combinations as selections without order.  
Prompt: If you pick 2 fruits from apple, banana, and cherry, how many different pairs can you make?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **combination** is a selection where order doesn’t matter.  
- Example: {apple, banana}, {apple, cherry}, {banana, cherry}.  
- Formula: nCr = n! / (r!(n – r)!).  

Combinations model choices like teams, committees, or lottery tickets.

## Basics of Probability

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects counting to chance.  
Prompt: What is the probability of rolling a 4 on a fair die?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Probability** = (favorable outcomes) ÷ (total outcomes).  
- Example: Die has 6 sides, 1 is a 4 → probability = 1/6.  
Combinatorics helps us count possible outcomes to calculate probabilities.

## Probability Models

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how probability predicts events.  
Prompt: If you flip a coin 100 times, how many heads do you expect?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Probability models describe long-term patterns:  
- Coin flips → expect about 50% heads.  
- Dice rolls → each number about 1/6 of the time.  
They help connect randomness to predictable averages.

## Real-Life Applications

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects combinatorics and probability to daily life.  
Prompt: Where do you see chance and counting rules outside of math class?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Games of chance: cards, dice, lotteries.  
- Security: PIN codes and passwords.  
- Science: genetics and probability of traits.  
- Business: risk analysis and forecasts.  

Combinatorics and probability help us plan for uncertainty and understand the role of chance.
