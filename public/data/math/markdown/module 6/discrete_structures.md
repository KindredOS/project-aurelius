## What Are Discrete Structures?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces discrete structures as the math of separate, countable things.  
Prompt: How is counting people different from measuring water in a bottle?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Discrete structures** deal with objects that can be counted one by one, like people, coins, or computers. Unlike continuous math (like measuring distance or area), discrete math focuses on steps, choices, and finite sets of possibilities.

## Sets and Elements

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains sets as collections of items.  
Prompt: What’s an example of a set you use in everyday life?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **set** is a collection of objects, called elements.  
- Example: The set of vowels = {a, e, i, o, u}.  
- Example: The set of days in a week.  

Sets help us group and organize data.

## Logic and Truth Tables

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces logic as the foundation of decision-making.  
Prompt: What does it mean if a statement is “true” or “false”?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Logic studies statements that can be true or false.  
- Example: “2 + 2 = 4” is true.  
- Example: “7 is even” is false.  

**Truth tables** show how combinations of statements lead to true/false outcomes. Logic powers algorithms and computer science.

## Graphs and Networks

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains graphs as nodes and connections.  
Prompt: How could you show the connections between friends in a social network?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **graph** is a structure made of nodes (points) and edges (connections).  
- Example: Cities connected by roads.  
- Example: People connected by friendships.  

Graph theory models relationships and systems in math and technology.

## Decision Trees

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how to organize decisions.  
Prompt: How could you map out the steps of choosing dinner at a restaurant?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **decision tree** maps out choices and their possible outcomes.  
- Example: Pizza or salad → add toppings or sides → total cost.  
Decision trees help plan strategies and analyze options step by step.
