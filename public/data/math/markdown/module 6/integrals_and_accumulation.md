## What Is an Integral?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces integrals as the opposite of derivatives.  
Prompt: If a derivative tells us the speed at a moment, what might an integral tell us about the whole trip?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

An **integral** measures the accumulation of change.  
- Derivative → instantaneous rate.  
- Integral → total amount over an interval.  

Integrals add up infinitely small pieces to find an area, a total, or an accumulation.

## Area Under a Curve

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects integrals to geometry.  
Prompt: How can you estimate the area under a curve using rectangles?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

An integral finds the exact area under a curve between two points.  
- Rectangles can approximate the area.  
- As the rectangles get thinner, the estimate improves.  
- The exact area is given by the **definite integral**.  

This is the foundation of calculus as summing infinitely small slices.

## Indefinite and Definite Integrals

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains the two main types of integrals.  
Prompt: What’s the difference between finding an antiderivative and finding the area under a curve?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- **Indefinite integral**: the general antiderivative, written ∫f(x)dx.  
- **Definite integral**: the total accumulation from a to b, written ∫ₐᵇ f(x)dx.  

Definite integrals calculate actual values, while indefinite integrals give general formulas.

## The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects derivatives and integrals.  
Prompt: How are differentiation and integration two sides of the same coin?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

The **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus** says differentiation and integration are inverses:  
- If F′(x) = f(x), then ∫ₐᵇ f(x)dx = F(b) – F(a).  
It unites the ideas of rate of change and accumulation into one powerful framework.

## Applications of Integrals

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows how integrals are used in real life.  
Prompt: Where might we need to add up lots of small changes to find a total?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Physics: distance from velocity, work, and energy.  
- Economics: total cost or revenue over time.  
- Biology: growth of populations.  
- Engineering: material stress and fluid flow.  

Integrals help us calculate totals where simple formulas won’t do.
