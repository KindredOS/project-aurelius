## What Is Algorithmic Thinking?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces algorithmic thinking as step-by-step problem solving.  
Prompt: How is following a recipe like solving a math problem?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

**Algorithmic thinking** is the process of breaking a problem into clear, ordered steps that can be followed to reach a solution.  
- Example: Long division follows a sequence of steps.  
- Example: A recipe lists steps to make a dish.  

Algorithms help us solve problems consistently and efficiently.

## Flowcharts and Pseudocode

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces tools for representing algorithms.  
Prompt: Why is it helpful to write down or draw the steps of a process?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

We can represent algorithms with:  
- **Flowcharts**: diagrams with arrows showing decisions and actions.  
- **Pseudocode**: simple, structured writing that outlines the logic.  

These tools make steps clear for humans and computers.

## Efficiency of Algorithms

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains why not all algorithms are equal.  
Prompt: What’s the difference between a long way and a shortcut in problem solving?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Some algorithms solve problems faster or with fewer steps.  
- Example: Multiplying instead of repeated addition.  
- Example: Searching with a “divide and conquer” method instead of checking everything.  

Efficiency matters in math, science, and computer science.
