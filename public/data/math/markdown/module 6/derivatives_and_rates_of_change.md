## What Is a Derivative?

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces derivatives as a way to measure change.  
Prompt: How is checking your exact speed on a speedometer different from finding your average speed on a trip?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

A **derivative** tells us the instantaneous rate of change of a function at a specific point.  
- Example: If distance changes with time, the derivative is velocity.  
- Symbol: f′(x) or dy/dx.  

Derivatives zoom in to describe change at one moment.

## Slope of a Tangent Line

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section connects derivatives to slopes on graphs.  
Prompt: What does the slope of a curve at a point tell us?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

The derivative at a point equals the slope of the tangent line to the curve there.  
- A positive slope means the function is increasing.  
- A negative slope means it’s decreasing.  
- Zero slope means a flat spot (possible maximum or minimum).  

Tangent lines visualize how fast a function is changing.

## Rules of Differentiation

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section introduces the basic rules.  
Prompt: How can shortcuts make finding derivatives faster than limits?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

Some key rules:  
- Power Rule: d/dx [xⁿ] = n·xⁿ⁻¹  
- Constant Rule: d/dx [c] = 0  
- Sum Rule: d/dx [f(x) + g(x)] = f′(x) + g′(x)  

These rules let us calculate derivatives quickly without always returning to limits.

## Higher-Order Derivatives

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section explains derivatives of derivatives.  
Prompt: What might the second derivative of a position function represent?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- The **first derivative** gives velocity (rate of change of position).  
- The **second derivative** gives acceleration (rate of change of velocity).  

Higher-order derivatives describe how rates themselves are changing.

## Applications of Derivatives

[Prompt Wrap Start]  
This section shows where derivatives are useful.  
Prompt: Where might you use derivatives in real life?  
[Prompt Wrap End]

- Physics: speed, acceleration, motion.  
- Economics: marginal cost and marginal profit.  
- Biology: population growth rates.  
- Engineering: stress and load changes in structures.  

Derivatives model change in every scientific and practical field.
