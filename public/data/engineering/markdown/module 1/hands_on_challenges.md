## Introduction to Hands-On Challenges

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces the value of hands-on learning through classroom engineering challenges. Prompt: Why do you think building things helps you learn?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Hands-on projects let students turn ideas into real experiments. When you build something, test it, and revise it, you learn how science and engineering apply in the real world. These challenges teach creativity, teamwork, and problem-solving—all while having fun.

## Rubber Band Car

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains how to design and test a rubber band-powered car. Prompt: How can you make your car go farther using only a rubber band?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Build a small car that moves using the energy stored in a stretched rubber band. As the band unwinds, it powers the wheels. Students can experiment with wheel size, axle materials, and band tension to improve performance and distance.

## Vector Walk

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces vector math through a kinesthetic activity. Prompt: What does it mean to move in a direction with both distance and angle?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Using ropes and directional cards, students walk vectors on the ground. Each step follows a set direction and distance. This challenge shows how combined movements create a final result—and helps teach direction, magnitude, and coordinates.

## Build a Force Meter

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section describes how to build a simple device to measure force. Prompt: How can you measure how hard something is pulling or pushing?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Students use springs, rubber bands, and scales to create a simple force meter. They can calibrate it using known weights and then test it with different loads. This teaches about force, elasticity, and measurement accuracy.

## Marshmallow Towers

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores balance and design through a classic building challenge. Prompt: How can you build a tall tower that doesn’t fall over?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Using marshmallows and spaghetti, students build the tallest stable structure they can. This challenge introduces concepts like load distribution, structural design, and center of gravity. It’s also a great teamwork test.

## Bridge Challenge

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section focuses on strength and stability in bridge-building. Prompt: What makes a bridge strong enough to hold weight?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Students design and build a bridge out of paper, cardboard, or craft sticks. Then they test it by adding weight until it collapses. They explore tension, compression, and efficient structure shapes like trusses and arches.

\[interactive element]

## Egg Drop

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces energy absorption and impact protection. Prompt: How can you protect a falling egg from breaking?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Students design a capsule to keep an egg safe when dropped from a height. They use materials like straws, cotton, and cardboard. This teaches about gravity, momentum, shock absorption, and creative problem-solving.

## Real-World Engineering Design

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section encourages students to invent their own challenge. Prompt: What’s a problem in your school or community that you could try to solve with a design?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

After trying guided challenges, students brainstorm their own real-world engineering projects. They can identify a problem, plan a solution, build a prototype, and test it. This final challenge encourages leadership, creativity, and using the engineering design process independently.
