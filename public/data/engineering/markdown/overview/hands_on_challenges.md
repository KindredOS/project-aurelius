## Hands-On Challenges

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces the idea of learning by doing through engineering-focused classroom activities.
**Prompt:** Why is hands-on learning important when studying engineering?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Hands-on learning helps students explore engineering concepts in a practical, fun way. Instead of just reading or watching, students build, test, and improve real projects. It encourages problem-solving, teamwork, and creativity while helping concepts stick.

## Tower Building Challenge

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section describes a classic engineering activity involving building tall, stable structures.
**Prompt:** What materials would you use to build the tallest tower—and how would you keep it from falling?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

In this challenge, students use simple materials like paper, tape, straws, or spaghetti to build the tallest freestanding tower. The goal is to think like structural engineers: balance weight, height, and stability.

## Budget Constraints Challenge

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores the importance of working within real-world limits.
**Prompt:** How would you design something great with limited materials or money?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Engineers often work with limited resources. This challenge gives students a set budget (in fake currency) and a price list for supplies. They must plan, design, and build something that meets the goal without going over budget.

## Invention Design Challenge

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces open-ended invention tasks that inspire creativity.
**Prompt:** If you could invent anything to solve a real-world problem, what would it be?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Students brainstorm problems in their community or daily lives, then invent a solution. They sketch ideas, build models, and present their inventions. It promotes empathy, innovation, and engineering design thinking.

## Rube Goldberg Machines

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces playful complexity in engineering.
**Prompt:** What’s the most creative way to complete a simple task using a chain reaction?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Rube Goldberg machines are silly yet brilliant contraptions that use many steps to do something simple. Students use ramps, pulleys, dominoes, and more to create chain reactions. It builds understanding of energy transfer and motion.

## Bridge Building Challenge

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores structural engineering and load-bearing design.
**Prompt:** What shapes make bridges strong—and why do engineers test them?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Students use materials like popsicle sticks or cardboard to build bridges that can hold weight. They test their designs by adding weights until the bridge breaks. This teaches about tension, compression, and structural strength.

## Redesign and Improve

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section focuses on the idea that failure is part of the process.
**Prompt:** How can failure help you build something better the next time?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

After completing a challenge, students reflect on what worked and what didn’t. Then they redesign and rebuild. This step teaches resilience, iteration, and the engineering mindset: improve, adapt, and try again.
