## Engineering Design Process

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces the idea that engineering is a process, not just a product.
**Prompt:** Why is it helpful to follow a step-by-step process when solving problems?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

The engineering design process helps guide teams from a problem to a solution. It’s not about guessing—it’s about thinking things through, trying ideas, learning from mistakes, and improving designs over time.

\[interactive element]

---

## Step 1: Ask & Define the Problem

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section focuses on identifying the real challenge.
**Prompt:** Why is it important to clearly define a problem before solving it?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Engineers start by asking questions and understanding the need. What are the limits? Who is it for? A well-defined problem makes it easier to create useful and focused solutions.

\[interactive element]

---

## Step 2: Imagine & Brainstorm

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section highlights creativity and idea generation.
**Prompt:** What are some ways to come up with lots of different ideas?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

In this step, engineers brainstorm possible solutions. No idea is too wild at first! Thinking freely helps teams consider new perspectives. More ideas mean more chances to find a great one.

\[interactive element]

---

## Step 3: Plan & Choose a Solution

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section shows how engineers choose the best idea and map it out.
**Prompt:** What makes one design idea better than another?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

After brainstorming, it’s time to pick the most promising solution and plan how to build it. Engineers consider things like cost, time, safety, and how well it meets the need. They sketch, list materials, and prepare to build.

\[interactive element]

---

## Step 4: Create & Build a Prototype

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains why building early versions is important.
**Prompt:** Why do engineers make prototypes before building the final version?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Prototypes are rough versions of a design. They help engineers try out their ideas, learn what works, and fix problems. Prototyping saves time and helps make better final products.

\[interactive element]

---

## Step 5: Test & Evaluate

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section teaches the importance of testing designs.
**Prompt:** How do engineers know if their solution really works?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Testing shows what’s working and what needs fixing. Engineers gather data and feedback, then use it to improve their design. Testing isn’t just about success—it’s about learning and improving.

\[interactive element]

---

## Step 6: Improve & Redesign

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section focuses on iteration and problem-solving.
**Prompt:** Why do engineers go back and make changes even after testing?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Almost no design is perfect the first time. Engineers use what they learn to make better versions. They redesign, rebuild, and retest until they have something strong, safe, and effective.

\[interactive element]

---

## Step 7: Communicate & Share Results

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section highlights teamwork and communication.
**Prompt:** Why is it important to share your process and results with others?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Engineers present their solutions through presentations, diagrams, or reports. Sharing helps others learn from your work, give feedback, or build on your ideas. Communication is key to progress.

\[interactive element]

---

## Design Is a Loop, Not a Line

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section reminds students that design is ongoing.
**Prompt:** How does repeating steps in the design process make your final product better?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Design doesn’t always follow a straight path. Engineers often loop back to earlier steps as they learn new things. This flexibility helps make smarter, stronger solutions over time.
