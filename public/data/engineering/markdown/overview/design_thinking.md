## Design Thinking & Systems

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section introduces design thinking and why it matters in engineering.
**Prompt:** Why do engineers start by thinking about the people who will use what they build?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Design thinking is a way of solving problems that starts with people. Engineers use empathy to understand what users need, brainstorm creative solutions, and test their ideas. It helps make designs that really work in real life.

## Steps in Design Thinking

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section outlines the main phases of the design thinking process.
**Prompt:** Which step in the process do you think is the most important, and why?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Design thinking usually includes five steps: Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, and Test. These steps guide engineers to learn about the user, clearly state the problem, come up with ideas, build quick versions, and test them to improve.

## Systems Thinking

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores the idea of viewing problems as part of a larger system.
**Prompt:** How can changing one part of a system affect the rest?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Systems thinking helps engineers understand how different parts of a system connect. A small change in one part—like fixing a water pipe—can affect the whole system. Engineers must think about how everything works together.

## Modeling Solutions

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explains why engineers build models and simulations.
**Prompt:** Why is it helpful to build a model before creating the real thing?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Models help engineers test ideas without wasting time or materials. They can be physical (like a cardboard model) or digital (like a computer simulation). Modeling shows what might work or fail before the real thing is built.

## Prototyping and Feedback

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section emphasizes early testing and learning from feedback.
**Prompt:** How can testing and feedback make a design better?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Engineers build prototypes to test their ideas quickly. Getting feedback from users or teammates helps find problems early. The goal isn’t to be perfect the first time—it’s to improve with each try.

## Real-World Applications

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section gives examples of how design and systems thinking are used in the world.
**Prompt:** Can you think of a product or system that works well because it was designed with people in mind?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Smartphones, public parks, and even school buildings are better when engineers think about how people will use them. Real-world design often includes features that make life easier, safer, or more fun because they started with the user first.

## Design for Equity

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section explores how design thinking can include everyone.
**Prompt:** What does it mean to design something that works for all people, not just some?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

Inclusive design makes sure everyone can use what engineers create. That means thinking about people with disabilities, different cultures, and all kinds of needs. Equity in design means fairness, not one-size-fits-all.

## Your Design Mindset

\[Prompt Wrap Start]
This section encourages students to see themselves as designers.
**Prompt:** What’s a problem you would like to solve by designing something new?
\[Prompt Wrap End]

If you’ve ever fixed something, made a plan, or improved an idea—you’ve already used design thinking. Everyone has the ability to look at a problem, imagine something better, and build a solution. You are a designer!
